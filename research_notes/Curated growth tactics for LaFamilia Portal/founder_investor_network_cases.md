Method note: in this cloud session every direct page fetch (ycombinator.com, techcrunch.com, endeavor.org, latitud.com, a16z.com, medium.com, wikipedia.org, df.cl, navalmanack.com, thenextweb.com, roundfunded.com, boringbusinessnerd.com) was blocked by network policy, so all findings below come from search-engine result summaries and snippets of those pages rather than full-page reads. ## Key Question 1: How does YC Bookface drive founder-to-founder and founder-to-investor interaction, and what usage stats exist?

### Takeaway
Bookface is a login-only, batch-gated bundle (profiles/directory, forum, investor database with founder-written reviews, vendor deals, internal tools) whose "growth" is purely a by-product of YC admissions; the investor side is governed by software-generated Demo Day invitations weighted by prior YC investment history, an algorithmic Investor Day matching system, and the public Handshake Deal Protocol that forces investors to commit unambiguously and on a deadline. YC publishes very few usage statistics for Bookface itself.

### Cited Findings
- YC describes Bookface as the platform founders use to connect to one another, "imagine a combination of Facebook, Quora, and LinkedIn." — [YC: What Happens at YC](https://ycombinator.com/about)
- Bookface is a login-only, private community for YC founders; accounts go to founders of accepted companies and are kept for alumni after the batch ends; it is "a benefit of acceptance, not a preview." — [Round Funded explainer](https://www.roundfunded.com/en/blogs/yc-bookface-explained-2026) (commercial competitor, treat framing as promotional)
- Bookface bundles five things in one login: founder profiles, a forum, vendor deals, an investor database, and internal tools/resources; the forum is where founders post "questions, intro requests, and polls to the rest of the network." — [Round Funded explainer](https://www.roundfunded.com/en/blogs/yc-bookface-explained-2026)
- YC's homepage as archived in May 2025 described the YC Investor Database as "profiles and reviews of more than 50,000 startup investors"; the current homepage no longer carries that line. — [Round Funded explainer](https://www.roundfunded.com/en/blogs/yc-bookface-explained-2026)
- Bookface "allows founders to rate their experiences with investors" (secondhand, podcast summary). — [High Stakes podcast](https://high-stakes.alitu.com/episode/a850fd54-8551-4d10-927c-9832d2bf1358)
- YC's own software page: founders can reach "over 9000 YC alumni" through Bookface; YC Deals offers "over 1000 deals from leading software companies" that companies report "to be worth in excess of $500,000." A Bookface landing snippet cites "over 5000 companies funded through YC." — [YC: Software at YC](https://ycombinator.com/software)
- YC built a public YC Founder Directory (searchable founder profiles) as a separate product from Bookface. — [YC blog: The YC Founder Directory](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/the-yc-founder-directory)
- Employees of YC companies originally did not have Bookface access; YC later launched separate access so employees could claim deals ("their employees don't have access to Bookface, where these exclusive deals are listed"). — [YC: Software at YC](https://ycombinator.com/software)
- Secondary accounts: founders describe an internal knowledge base of playbooks and templates tied to their batch, and "daily, founders post questions, and a network of experts readily offer advice, help, and connections"; a Lago cofounder said the platform answers "80% of the questions a founder can have" (opinion, reported secondhand). — [eChai: How Bookface helps early-stage founders](https://echai.ventures/startingup/r/how-bookface-yc-helps-early-stage-founders-thrive--k1-r187)
- Unverified user-generated claim: YC has removed founders from Bookface for posting internal information externally. — [Notion page "Founder Social Network"](https://loud-particle-7d0.notion.site/Founder-Social-Network-3a08d2b9402b8080a226c65686b54a03)
- Alumni Demo Day: active YC founders get an early look at each new batch before investors (secondhand). — [Round Funded explainer](https://www.roundfunded.com/en/blogs/yc-bookface-explained-2026); a Luma listing shows a "YC Next Alumni W26 Batch Demo Day - invitation only" event. — [Luma](https://luma.com/3g3rknzo)
- Demo Day audience: YC's FAQ says there is no standing investor list; "Invitations are software generated and based on recent investment history in YC startups," and investors can apply at ycombinator.com/demoday. The FAQ cites about 1,000 investors and media; the current Demo Day page says "an invite-only audience of approximately 1,500 investors and media." — [YC Demo Day FAQ](https://www.ycombinator.com/demoday/faq/); [YC Demo Day page](https://www.ycombinator.com/demoday/)
- AngelList's description: "YC hand-selects the investors who can attend Demo Day based on their investment track record, with special weight to investments in YC companies." — [AngelList blog: How AngelList GPs invest at YC Demo Day](https://blog.angellist.com/how-angellist-gps-invest-at-yc-demo-day)
- Sept 2024: Garry Tan said in-person Demo Day seats "will be limited and reserved for decision-making investors who have invested at least $50,000 into YC companies within the last two years." — [TechCrunch, 2024-09-25](https://techcrunch.com/2024/09/25/y-combinators-next-demo-day-will-include-in-person-seats-for-top-vcs-garry-tan-says/)
- Spring 2025 Demo Day: "more than 1,500 investors squeezed into a San Francisco venue." — [Alumni Ventures blog](https://www.av.vc/blog/decoding-ycs-s25-demo-day-trends-takeaways)
- Investor Day software (Aug 30, 2016, Finbarr Taylor): YC created a full day of 20-minute investor/startup meetings right after the two Demo Days, so each company could meet 15 investors in a day. Investors "liked" companies during Demo Day, then ranked them; each company ranked the investors that liked it; software generated many schedules and picked the best on meeting count and average quality. There were roughly three times as many investors as companies. "Over 80% of investors who wanted to attend and liked companies during Demo Day had meetings scheduled" and "more than 97% of founders and investors said they would do it again." — [YC blog: The Software Behind YC's Investor Day](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/investor-day-software)
- Handshake Deal Protocol (Paul Graham, 2013): a deal exists only once the investor states clear acceptance of a specific single-amount offer; no ranges ("an investor offering $50k to $150k doesn't qualify"), no conditions; it sets deadlines so an investor cannot delay acceptance indefinitely and creates a paper trail so YC "can track whether investors are breaking their verbal agreements." TechCrunch's W13 Demo Day coverage (March 2013) said all founders would adhere to the "recently composed" protocol. Business Insider later described a "Handshake" button for investors at Demo Day. — [YC: The Handshake Deal Protocol](https://ycombinator.com/handshake); [TechCrunch W13 Demo Day](https://techcrunch.com/2013/03/26/y-combinator-winter-2013-demo-day-batch-1/amp/); [Business Insider](https://businessinsider.in/silicon-valleys-top-startup-factory-has-a-clever-way-to-make-sure-investors-follow-through-on-handshake-deals/amp_articleshow/48549526.cms)
- YC runs a public Co-Founder Matching platform (launched 2021) as a separate funnel product. — [YC blog: Co-Founder Matching](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/co-founder-matching/)
- YC maintains a public filter page listing Hispanic/Latino-founded YC companies. — [YC: Hispanic/Latino founders](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/hispanic-latino-founders)

### Inferences
- Bookface's vetting is inherited: membership = admission to a batch, so there is no separate community admissions funnel to run; the mechanism that scales interaction is (a) a single searchable directory + forum where intro requests are the norm, and (b) reputational data flowing in one direction only (founders review investors, investors cannot see or respond publicly). A Latino portal can copy the asymmetric-review design to make investors accountable without opening the directory.
- The investor side is a two-gate system: invitation generated from observed behavior (did you actually invest in our companies in the last 2 years, at >= $50K) plus an explicit commitment protocol (Handshake). Both are copyable without YC's brand: track investor behavior inside the portal, and publish a commitment protocol with deadlines.
- Investor Day's design (mutual ranking, many-to-many assignment, 20-minute slots, ~3 investors per company) is the most concrete "deal-flow matching" spec found; 80%/97% satisfaction numbers suggest it is a proven template for a quarterly matching day.

### Gaps
- No published YC statistics on Bookface forum volume, response times, "ask YC" SLAs, or office-hours usage were found; YC does not appear to publish them.
- Could not confirm current size of the Investor Database (the 50,000 figure is from a May 2025 archive via a competitor's page).
- Could not read YC's own pages directly (network-blocked), so forum norms/rules text is unverified.

## Key Question 2: How did On Deck grow from 0 to thousands of fellows, what were the cohort mechanics and pricing, and why did it contract in 2022?

### Takeaway
On Deck started (2016-2019) as Erik Torenberg's invite-only dinner series, converted it into a paid, application-based 10-week Founder Fellowship (ODF) in 2019, then cloned the format into dozens of vertical fellowships during 2020-2021 on $20M (Series A, Mar 2021) and $40M (Tiger-led Series B) of venture money plus a planned $100M community-backed accelerator fund (ODX). When Tiger cut its ODX commitment from $65M to $10M and the 2022 market turned, On Deck laid off ~25% (May 2022) and ~33-50% (Aug 2022), sunset ODX, spun out its career programs, and refocused on ODF, which still exists on a pay-what-you-can (~$1,000 suggested) non-dilutive model.

### Cited Findings
- Origin: "When I first started On Deck as a dinner series for my talented friends, I couldn't have imagined it would turn into such an incredible community" (Torenberg); the dinners "turned out to be a hit. Soon enough, they spread to other cities." — [Erik Torenberg Substack: On Deck 2020 recap](https://eriktorenberg.substack.com/p/on-deck-2020-recap-and-looking-ahead)
- The Fellowship "was born: A 10-week program gathering 200+ talented founders"; the original Founders Fellowship included weekly dinners, each featuring fellows and invited guests. — [Erik Torenberg Substack: What's On Deck for On Deck](https://eriktorenberg.substack.com/p/whats-on-deck-for-on-deck)
- A 2019-era post describes an 8-week cohort of up to 100 people; the Second On Deck Fellowship was announced for October 2019. — [Medium: Announcing the Second On Deck Fellowship, Oct 2019](https://medium.com/the-on-deck-journal/announcing-the-second-on-deck-fellowship-october-2019-97d28e5c9843); [elev-x review](https://elev-x.com/news-insights/article-on-deck-fellowship-review/)
- Company launched June 2019 with a Founders Fellowship first, later adding niche vertical programs. — [TechCrunch, 2022-08-04](https://techcrunch.com/2022/08/04/on-deck-lays-off-a-third-of-staff-after-cutting-a-quarter-just-months-prior)
- Engagement claim in the Series A memo: of 2,000 people in the ODF Slack channel, "72% are active 3 months later" (self-reported); the memo also claimed "scaling communities is hard, but we think we've cracked the code." — [On Deck Series A investor memo via Alexander Jarvis](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/on-deck-series-a-investor-memo/)
- Referral engine: "over 2,500 direct referrals to ODF alone" (Packy McCormick memo, reposted by Torenberg). — [Not Boring: What's On Deck for On Deck, March 25, 2021](https://www.notboring.co/p/whats-on-deck-for-on-deck)
- Outcomes claimed: "at least 330 companies had been founded and collectively raised upwards of $250M pre-seed/seed funding during the On Deck Founder Fellowship, in just 18 months" (Series A memo); earlier site: "nearly $150M raised in Pre-Seed/Seed funding during ODF across the first three cohorts"; later statement: "650+ companies founded since late 2019" with fellows/alumni raising "$800 million+" (date not shown). — [Alexander Jarvis memo page](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/on-deck-series-a-investor-memo/); [ondeck.carrd.co](https://ondeck.carrd.co/)

- Pricing: the Flywheel interview describes an upfront fee ("tuition") of about $2,000 per fellow; older third-party reviews cite roughly $2,990; a 2022 Climate Tech fellowship was "a ten-week, $2,490 climate tech fellowship"; the current ODF page describes a non-dilutive, pay-what-you-can model with a suggested contribution of about $1,000 USD. — [The Flywheel interview](https://theflywheel.substack.com/p/interview-erik-torenberg-david-booth); [elev-x](https://elev-x.com/news-insights/article-on-deck-fellowship-review/); [TechCrunch 2022-08-04](https://techcrunch.com/2022/08/04/on-deck-lays-off-a-third-of-staff-after-cutting-a-quarter-just-months-prior); [startupintros.com](https://startupintros.com/resources/on-deck-founders-fellowship)

- ODX accelerator (announced Oct 2021): a planned "$100 million community-backed fund"; terms $125K for 7%; "ODX will be for those 100% ready to take the plunge and start a company." — [Erik Torenberg: Announcing ODX](https://eriktorenberg.substack.com/p/announcing-odx); [Thread](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1451262879655596032.html)

Severance: eight weeks, three months accelerated vesting, three months healthcare. — [TechCrunch 2022-08-04](https://techcrunch.com/2022/08/04/on-deck-lays-off-a-third-of-staff-after-cutting-a-quarter-just-months-prior); [Slashdot/Protocol](https://tech.slashdot.org/story/22/05/05/1934210/)

Without Tiger's infusion the company had been "spending right from its balance sheet," leaving "only nine months of runway." — [TechCrunch 2022-08-04](https://techcrunch.com/2022/08/04/on-deck-lays-off-a-third-of-staff-after-cutting-a-quarter-just-months-prior); [IBTimes](https://ibtimes.com/deck-cuts-staff-again-over-fractured-focus-during-2-year-growth-3598331)

Sept 2022: Torenberg stepped back from co-CEO to executive chairman after one year; David Booth became sole CEO. Accelerator terms shifted to $25,000 for 1% (up to 2.5%) versus the prior $125,000 for 7%; later ODX was sunset entirely ("will close after this last batch") with a shift toward pre-seed investing. — [TechCrunch 2022-08-04](https://techcrunch.com/2022/08/04/on-deck-lays-off-a-third-of-staff-after-cutting-a-quarter-just-months-prior); [TechCrunch ?p=2406739](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2406739); [Shawn Xu, Turning the Page at On Deck](https://medium.com/@shawnxu/turning-the-page-at-on-deck-32cdc93fb585)
- Axios analysis: On Deck "didn't want to charge fees for the accelerator, unlike its other programs," leaving less margin; TechCrunch: the company switched "back to revenue-generating programs instead of basing its entire future on the accelerator model." — [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2022/10/08/on-deck-startup-accelerator-slump); [TechCrunch ?p=2405954](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2405954)
- Insider lesson (former admissions employee, personal blog): "once we tied employee compensation to revenue and the size of the cohort, the quality bar started to slip." — [Boring Business Nerd](https://boringbusinessnerd.com/post/what-i-learned-working-in-a-hypergrowth-startup)
- Torenberg lesson (from Product Hunt, applied to On Deck): expansions "should make the core product stronger, or be uniquely enabled by it, rather than pull the company in other directions." — [The Flywheel interview](https://theflywheel.substack.com/p/interview-erik-torenberg-david-booth)
- Adjacent community-building guidance: First Round's 2019 piece (Bailey Richardson) advises "find the 100 who give a damn and don't worry about scaling"; Lenny's Newsletter guest post by David Spinks covers when to invest in community and how to scale. — [First Round Review](https://review.firstround.com/from-instant-pot-to-instagram-critical-lessons-in-startup-community-building.md); [Lenny's Newsletter: A founder's guide to community](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/building-community)

### Inferences
- Growth loop that worked: paid, application-based cohorts (~$2-3K) + weekly dinners/Slack + public outcome claims ($ raised by fellows) -> alumni referrals (2,500+ direct referrals) -> larger cohorts. The paid model funded operations without equity, kept applicants serious, and the scholarship fund preserved access.
- What broke: (1) cloning the format into many verticals diluted the brand and staff focus; (2) a free accelerator dependent on one anchor LP; (3) incentive misalignment (admissions comp tied to cohort size) eroding selectivity. These are the three failure modes a curated portal should design against: keep one flagship program, never let a single funder underwrite the core, and keep admissions comp independent of volume.
- (est.) The 72%-active-after-3-months Slack figure and 36-hour admissions decision are the two most copyable operating metrics.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm the exact ODF tuition in 2020-2021 from a primary source (figures range $2,000-$2,990 depending on source and year); the $2,490 figure is confirmed only for the 2022 Climate fellowship.
- No founder-written post-mortem by Torenberg or Booth was found; "lessons" are from interviews and press, not a dedicated retrospective. No Reforge or First Round analysis specifically of On Deck was found.
- Total number of distinct fellowships launched (reported elsewhere as 30+) could not be verified in these results.

## Key Question 3: How does Endeavor select entrepreneurs and mobilize mentors, and what engagement data has it published?

### Takeaway
Endeavor runs a multi-stage, mentor-staffed selection funnel (local screening -> second-opinion mentor reviews -> local panel -> International Selection Panel of six business leaders who must vote unanimously), selects fewer than ~7% of companies screened and only ~81 companies in 2024, takes no equity at selection (co-investing passively via Endeavor Catalyst), and runs its mentor network (3,500+ active mentors) on a volunteer, pay-it-forward "multiplier" norm; every selection step doubles as mentorship.

### Cited Findings
- The International Selection Panel (ISP) is "the culmination of a rigorous, multi-step selection process"; earlier steps include staff interviews and mentor-led reviews; "each step doubles as a mentorship opportunity." — [Endeavor 2024 Impact Report (PDF)](https://endeavor.org/wp-content/uploads/dlm_uploads/2025/03/IMPACTREPORT2024_comp.pdf); [Endeavor US: Selection as a Service](https://us.endeavor.org/?p=610)
- "Local mentors and Board Members interview, deliberate, and select candidates for an International Selection Panel"; ISPs are "made up of international business leaders who interview candidates and select new Endeavor Entrepreneurs by unanimous vote"; "each candidate is individually interviewed by six mentors, and all six must reach a unanimous decision." — [Endeavor US entrepreneur experience](https://us.endeavor.org/entrepreneur-experience/); [us.endeavor.org/?p=29](https://us.endeavor.org/?p=29)
- For second-opinion reviews "the Endeavor team scours its global network to find the best mentors to conduct second opinion review interviews." — [Endeavor Nigeria](https://nigeria.endeavor.org/?p=1090)
- Selectivity: "historically, fewer than 7% of the companies Endeavor screens get selected"; "in 2024, only 81 companies led by high-impact entrepreneurs were chosen to join Endeavor." — [endeavor.org/?p=428785](https://endeavor.org/?p=428785); [endeavor.org/?p=436140](https://endeavor.org/?p=436140)
- 2013 baseline: Endeavor had "screened more than 30,000 entrepreneurs and selected 726 individuals." — [CNBC press release, 2012/2013](https://www.cnbc.com/id/100132238)
- Panel cadence/size: the 96th ISP was held in Bali; a Sept 2025 ISP in Cambridge, UK; one 2025 panel selected "seven entrepreneurs leading five companies"; a Stellenbosch panel selected 17 new Endeavor Entrepreneurs. — [Endeavor: 96th ISP Bali](https://endeavor.org/stories/endeavor-96th-isp-bali/); [Endeavor 2025 impact report page](https://endeavor.org/stories/2025-impact-report/); [Endeavor Stellenbosch](https://endeavor.org/stories/17-new-endeavor-entrepreneurs-selected-in-stellenbosch/)
- Network scale (2024): "2,900+ Endeavor Entrepreneurs generated $75 billion in revenue and created 4.1M jobs in 2024" (another Endeavor PDF gives $67B annual revenue and 4.1M jobs; a later page gives $88.5B cumulative). — [endeavor.org/?p=425637](https://endeavor.org/?p=425637); [Endeavor Overview 2024 PDF](https://endeavor.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Endeavor-Overview_2024.pdf)
- Endeavor Catalyst (co-investment fund): "nearly 350 investments," "61 $1 billion+ unicorns," "27 exits." Endeavor "does not take equity as part of selection"; Catalyst "co-invests passively in qualified rounds." — [Endeavor 2024 Impact Report summary](https://endeavor.org/?p=425637); [Endeavor Atlanta services](https://endeavor-atlanta.squarespace.com/services-1)
- Mentor network: mentors are selected "from the 3500+ active Endeavor Mentor network"; the same page also says "a volunteer network of more than a thousand global and local business leaders" (figures conflict, undated). Network spans "30+ countries (and 60+ cities)" (Atlanta page) or "40 markets" (South Africa page). — [Endeavor mentors page](https://endeavor.org/global-community/mentors/); [Endeavor Atlanta](https://endeavor-atlanta.squarespace.com/services-1); [Endeavor South Africa](https://southafrica.endeavor.org/entrepreneur-experience/)
- Mentors "donate their time and expertise" (e.g., Chris Misner, Advisor of Roblox). — [Why Endeavor?](https://endeavor.org/?p=401837)
- Engagement data at local level: Endeavor Malaysia's "80+ mentors donated over 2,700 hours in 2024"; Endeavor Miami reports "$111.5M+ generated revenue, 1008+ jobs created" for supported companies in 2024; Endeavor Philippines 2023: $900M revenue, 66,000 jobs, $1B capital. — [Endeavor Malaysia 2024 Impact Report](https://malaysia.endeavor.org/?p=400504); [Endeavor Miami Impact Report 2024](https://endeavormiami.org/impact-report-2024/); [Tribune PH](https://tribune.net.ph/2024/12/30/endeavor-philippines-earns-900-m-revenue)
- "Multiplier effect" is the named norm: high-impact entrepreneurs "inspire, mentor and invest in other entrepreneurs." — [Endeavor Philippines Impact Report 2022](https://issuu.com/endeavorph/docs/endeavor_philippines_impact_report_2022)
- Case: a founder met a mentor at the Dublin ISP who later helped him close a $53M Series B. — [Endeavor 2024 Impact Report (PDF)](https://endeavor.org/wp-content/uploads/dlm_uploads/2025/03/IMPACTREPORT2024_comp.pdf)
- Local chapters are structured as "a Board of Directors, a small dedicated team, and a group of carefully selected peer entrepreneurs and mentors" (Canada; similar in Bulgaria, Nigeria). — [Endeavor Canada](https://canada.endeavor.org/?p=398872)
- Endeavor also invested in Latitud's 2022 seed round (see KQ4). — [Bloomberg Linea](https://www.bloomberglinea.com/2022/03/28/latitud-first-round-is-a-115-million-a16z-led-seed/)

### Inferences
- Endeavor's core trick is to make the selection process itself the engagement product for mentors: every panelist slot is a high-status, time-boxed volunteer role, and every candidate interview is a mentoring touchpoint, so selection generates mentor hours rather than consuming them.
- Unanimity by six panelists is an explicit quality control that caps cohort size naturally (~81 companies/year globally across ~8-10 panels, est.) and makes the "Endeavor Entrepreneur" label scarce enough to be worth volunteering for.
- Chapter structure (board + small staff + peer entrepreneurs) is a franchise model for geographic expansion that a Latino portal could mirror city by city (Miami, Mexico City, Bogota, etc.).

### Gaps
- No global published metrics on mentor engagement (sessions per entrepreneur, response times, hours) were found; only local chapters (Malaysia) publish hours.
- Could not read the full 2024 Impact Report PDF (blocked), so detailed selection funnel numbers (applicants per year, ISP count in 2024) are missing.

## Key Question 4: What has Latitud published about its community model, fellowship and LatAm growth?

### Takeaway
Latitud (founded 2020 by Brian Requarth, Gina Gotthilf and Yuri Danilchenko) grew an equity-free, application-only Fellowship from a November 2020 launch to 700-800+ founders by 2022-2025, raised an $11.5M a16z/NFX-led seed in March 2022 on the strength of community traction (800+ fellows who had raised $250M at $1.5B+ combined valuation), added an Angel Fellowship for unicorn founders, and in 2025 narrowed to a highly selective San Francisco pre-founder fellowship (8 per cohort from 700+ applicants, $62.5K for 2.5%).

### Cited Findings
- Latitud was founded in 2020 by Brian Requarth, Gina Gotthilf and Yuri Danichenko; Gotthilf was an early employee/VP Growth at Duolingo; Requarth sold VivaReal for $550M and paid "more than $100 million in capital gains taxes due to incorporation errors made early on," the rationale for Latitud's company-formation product. — [TechCrunch: Latitud raises $11.5M](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2289987)

## Key Question 5: How did AngelList seed investor and founder liquidity early (2010-2013), including the manual intros Naval and Nivi did?

### Takeaway
AngelList began (Feb-Apr 2010) as a hand-curated email list: Naval Ravikant and Babak Nivi screened startups from their Venture Hacks audience, screened the ~25-50 angels who received them, and emailed a weekly digest of three pitches; investors replied asking for intros, which the founders brokered by hand. Volume data was self-reported by both sides. By 2011 it had 2,500 investors, 500 startups and 12,500 intros; by April 2013 it was driving 500-700 intros/week and ~$10M/month raised, then layered on Syndicates (2013) so well-known angels could lead and earn carry, and later split Talent out as Wellfound (Nov 2022).

### Cited Findings
- Venture Hacks began April 2007 when Ravikant and Nivi started blogging about fundraising; AngelList is described as the "productization" of that resource. — [Vator: AngelList pivoted four times](https://vator.tv/n/16cd); [TechCrunch ?p=490380](https://techcrunch.com/?p=490380)
- Launch date conflict: launched in a Venture Hacks blog post on February 20, 2010 (earliest version reportedly called "Venture Hacks Dealflow Network") vs. Wikipedia's April 22, 2010. — [Insights4VC](https://insights4vc.substack.com/p/how-did-angellist-democratize-seed); [Wikipedia: AngelList](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AngelList)
- Early mechanics: "Dubbed StartupList, Venture Hacks will start sending a weekly Email digest featuring three startup pitches to angel investors." — [TechCrunch ?p=490380](https://techcrunch.com/?p=490380)
- "AngelList is a curated email distribution list of angel and seed stage investors. Nivi and Naval screen the companies / deals that get pushed out to this list, and they screen the investors for inclusion on the receiving end." — [The Next Web, Oct 4 2010: The quiet rise of AngelList](https://thenextweb.com/news/the-quiet-rise-of-angellist)
- Starting list size conflict: "a list of 25 investors with whom they would share interesting companies" vs. "50 angel investors who intended to invest US$80 million that year." — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AngelList); [Wikiwand mirror](https://wikiwand.com/en/AngelList)
- Ravikant: "I called up my partner in Venture Hacks, Nivi and suggested they put up a list"; he framed AngelList as "the match.com of funding" (Feb 2011). — [Navalmanack: The story of AngelList](https://navalmanack.com/secret-sections/the-story-of-angellist); [The Next Web, Feb 17 2011](https://thenextweb.com/news/naval-ravikant-angellist-the-match-com-of-funding-interview)
- Feb 15, 2011 (Nivi): AngelList had "helped orchestrate funding for 219 start-ups from 384 investors," an estimate because "AngelList doesn't formally track the investments that occur due to those intros." — [Venture Hacks: Active AngelList](https://venturehacks.com/active-angellist)
- July 25, 2011 (Nivi): after ~1.5 years, "An investor has asked for an intro to a startup on AngelList over 8,000 times... this is the data that the startups and investors have kindly reported." — [Venture Hacks: Centi-sesquicentennial](https://venturehacks.com/centi-sesquicentennial)
- 2011 Yearbook (Jan 2012): "500 startups joined with more than 10 introductions facilitated by each, 2,500 investors joined the community, resulting in a total of 12,500 introductions during 2011." — [TechCrunch, 2012-01-29](https://techcrunch.com/2012/01/29/angellist-launches-2011-yearbook-500-startups-2500-investors-12500-introductions/)
- April 2012: "978 startups have found some form of investment since 2010," 48 acquisitions. — [TechCrunch, 2012-04-20](https://techcrunch.com/2012/04/20/the-power-of-angellist-revealed-in-its-new-look-978-done-deals-48-acquisitions)
- Dec 2012: AngelList helped raise $12M over 30 days through its online investing feature (via SecondMarket partnership, a workaround for securities regulation). — [The Next Web, 2012-12-26](https://thenextweb.com/news/angellist-helps-raise-12-million-over-30-days)
- April 2013: "500 to 700 introductions between startups and accredited investors per week," "on average $10 million per month" raised; $6.7M raised for startups via Invest Online before SEC approval; platform opened to more companies. — [TechCrunch, 2013-04-24](https://techcrunch.com/2013/04/24/after-raising-6-7m-for-startups-and-winning-sec-approval-angellist-opens-up-investment-platform-to-more-companies)
- Syndicates: private beta reported July 27, 2013, letting any accredited investor "create, lead and collect carry for a fund of angel money for a specific startup"; SEC no-action letter in 2013; Sept 23, 2013 AngelList allowed startups to publicly advertise fundraising (post-JOBS Act). First year of syndicates: about $87M in seed deals, volume up more than 300% between February and October 2014; early syndicate leads included Tim Ferriss and Gil Penchina. — [TechCrunch, 2013-07-27](https://techcrunch.com/2013/07/27/angellist-syndicates-lets-angels-get-carry-for-helping-a-startup-raise-money/); [The Next Web, 2013-09-23](https://thenextweb.com/news/angellist-now-allows-startups-to-publicly-advertise-fundraising-efforts); [Fortune, 2014-11-13](https://fortune.com/2014/11/13/angellist-ceo-naval-ravikant-disruptor/)
- Full venture funds launched May 2017; Syndicates launched in India Jan 2018. — [TechCrunch 2017-05-16](https://techcrunch.com/2017/05/16/angellist-just-launched-full-fledged-venture-funds/feed); [TechCrunch 2018-01-15](https://techcrunch.com/2018/01/15/angellist-launches-syndicates-in-india)
- Nov 14, 2022: AngelList Talent spun out as an independent company, Wellfound; AngelList Venture became simply "AngelList"; domain move from angel.co to wellfound.com finished by March 2023; codebase, domain and infrastructure were forked. — [AngelList blog: Reintroducing AngelList](https://blog.angellist.com/reintroducing-angellist); [Wellfound: About](https://wellfound.com/about); [AngelList blog: How we forked AngelList](https://www.angellist.com/blog/how-we-forked-angellist)
- AngelList also runs "Demo Day funds" and GPs invest at YC Demo Day. — [AngelList Meridian: Demo Day funds](https://meridian.angellist.com/demo-day-funds)

### Inferences
- The 2010 launch pattern is the clearest "concierge liquidity" template found: start with an existing trusted audience (Venture Hacks readers), hand-screen both sides, email a fixed small number of deals (3/week) to a small vetted list (25-50 angels), and let investors pull ("ask for an intro") rather than letting founders push. Only once intro volume was self-reporting at thousands did product automation follow.
- Publishing aggregate activity (219 funded, 8,000 intros, 12,500 intros, 978 deals) was itself a growth mechanic: it was the social proof that pulled new investors in while screening was still manual.
- The later shifts (Syndicates, funds, Talent spin-out) show AngelList moving from intro-brokering (hard to monetize, hard to measure) to capital-moving products with fees/carry; the 2022 fork is a focus move similar to On Deck's but executed from strength.

### Gaps
- No primary source detailed the exact screening criteria Nivi/Naval used in 2010 or how many startups applied vs. were sent out; the Navalmanack and Venture Hacks launch posts could not be opened.
- No source on investor response-rate norms or SLAs inside early AngelList.

## Key Question 6: What do these organizations do to get investors to actually respond to founders?

### Takeaway
None of the organizations publishes an investor response SLA; instead they use five structural levers: (1) admission of investors based on observed behavior (YC: software-generated Demo Day invites from recent YC investment history; $50K-in-2-years threshold for in-person seats); (2) founder-written investor reviews visible only to founders (YC Investor Database); (3) commitment protocols with deadlines and a paper trail (YC Handshake Protocol); (4) mutual-ranking matching that only schedules meetings where both sides opted in (YC Investor Day: 20-minute slots, 80%+ of willing investors matched); and (5) investor-pull rather than founder-push (AngelList's "investor asks for an intro" model; Endeavor's mentor-staffed panels where investors/mentors come to the founders).

### Cited Findings
- YC Demo Day invitations "are software generated and based on recent investment history in YC startups"; investors can apply. — [YC Demo Day FAQ](https://www.ycombinator.com/demoday/faq/)
- In-person Demo Day seats reserved for investors "who have invested at least $50,000 into YC companies within the last two years" (Garry Tan, Sept 2024). — [TechCrunch 2024-09-25](https://techcrunch.com/2024/09/25/y-combinators-next-demo-day-will-include-in-person-seats-for-top-vcs-garry-tan-says/)
- Handshake Deal Protocol: no ranges, no conditions, deadlines so investors can't delay indefinitely, digital paper trail so YC "can track whether investors are breaking their verbal agreements." — [YC Handshake](https://ycombinator.com/handshake); [Business Insider](https://businessinsider.in/silicon-valleys-top-startup-factory-has-a-clever-way-to-make-sure-investors-follow-through-on-handshake-deals/amp_articleshow/48549526.cms)
- Investor Day: mutual "like" + rank, 20-minute meetings, 15 per company, 80%+ of willing investors scheduled, 97%+ would repeat. — [YC blog: Investor Day software](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/investor-day-software)
- YC Investor Database holds "profiles and reviews" of investors written by founders (50,000+ as of May 2025 archive); founders "rate their experiences with investors." — [Round Funded](https://www.roundfunded.com/en/blogs/yc-bookface-explained-2026); [High Stakes podcast](https://high-stakes.alitu.com/episode/a850fd54-8551-4d10-927c-9832d2bf1358)
- AngelList 2010-2011: investors asked for intros (8,000+ requests by July 2011), i.e., investor-initiated contact; founders' deals were pre-screened before reaching the list. — [Venture Hacks](https://venturehacks.com/centi-sesquicentennial); [The Next Web 2010](https://thenextweb.com/news/the-quiet-rise-of-angellist)
- AngelList Syndicates gave angels carry for leading, i.e., a financial incentive to engage and respond. — [TechCrunch 2013-07-27](https://techcrunch.com/2013/07/27/angellist-syndicates-lets-angels-get-carry-for-helping-a-startup-raise-money/)
- Endeavor: six-mentor unanimous panels mean senior business leaders/investors are the ones interviewing founders; a mentor met at an ISP later helped close a $53M Series B. — [us.endeavor.org/?p=29](https://us.endeavor.org/?p=29); [Endeavor 2024 Impact Report](https://endeavor.org/wp-content/uploads/dlm_uploads/2025/03/IMPACTREPORT2024_comp.pdf)
- Techstars: mentees score mentors through a "Mentor Deck" that informs training and recognizes top mentors; annual public "All-Star Mentors" lists (2022-2025). — [Techstars Mentor FAQs](https://www.techstars.com/mentor-faq); [Techstars: 2025 All-Star Mentors](https://www.techstars.com/blog/impact/meet-the-2025-techstars-all-star-mentors)
- Techstars Mentor Manifesto (Brad Feld, David Cohen) codifies norms: "give first," i.e., "putting energy into a system without knowing what you're going to get back"; mentors help "not because they expect something in return." — [Techstars: Mentor Manifesto](https://www.techstars.com/blog/founder-advice/mentor-manifesto); [Feld: Mentor Manifesto detail](https://feld.com/archives/2018/01/techstars-mentor-manifesto-detail)
- Techstars Universe (private platform for founders, mentors, staff) includes tools for "building an investor pipeline and managing a raise" and access to "over 15,000 entrepreneurs, mentors, and investors." — [Techstars help: resources for alumni](https://help.techstars.com/support/solutions/articles/4000215875-what-resources-are-available-for-techstars-alumni-); [Techstars for alumni](https://www.techstars.com/for/alumni)
- Platanus Ventures created "Kernel," an angel investor club/training program with "25 cupos por cohorte" and three to four generations per year, as a vehicle to pool angel capital and co-invest in the accelerator's portfolio. — [Diario Financiero](https://www.df.cl/df-lab/innovacion-y-startups/platanus-ventures-internacionaliza-sus-hackathones-lanza-club-de); [Ecosistema Startup](https://ecosistemastartup.com/?p=83003)

### Inferences
- The common thread: investors respond when (a) their access is contingent on demonstrated behavior, (b) founders can see each other's experiences with them, and (c) the ask is bounded (20 minutes, a yes/no with a deadline). A Latino portal can implement all three in software: behavior-gated investor tier, founder-only investor reviews, and a Handshake-style commitment button with deadlines and visible follow-through rates.
- Public recognition (Techstars All-Star Mentors, Endeavor's "multiplier" stories) is the non-financial lever; carry (AngelList Syndicates, Platanus Kernel) is the financial one. Both are compatible with curation.

### Gaps
- No organization was found to publish investor response rates or response-time SLAs; this appears to be an unexploited differentiator.
- Could not verify YC's internal rules about how investor reviews are moderated.

## Key Question 7: Techstars, 500 Global, and LatAm-focused communities (Platanus and others)

### Takeaway
Techstars and 500 run accelerator-gated alumni networks with named norms (Give First / #500Strong), mentor scoring and public mentor recognition; Platanus Ventures (Chile, launched March 3, 2020) is the most documented LatAm analogue: Spanish-language, CTO-required, ~0.9% selection, US$200K for 7% (2026 terms), biweekly "Coliseo" office hours, a regional hackathon circuit as top-of-funnel, and a 25-seat angel club (Kernel) to build its investor side.

### Cited Findings
- Techstars Universe: "private platform for Techstars founders, mentors, and staff"; network "of over 15,000 entrepreneurs, mentors, and investors"; mentor counts vary by year: "over 8500 active mentors" (2020), "5,000-strong" (2023), "3,100+" (2025), "1,500+" (2026). — [Techstars help](https://help.techstars.com/support/solutions/articles/4000215875-what-resources-are-available-for-techstars-alumni-); [Techstars DEI newsroom 2020](https://www.techstars.com/newsroom/diversity-equity-inclusion-techstars-june-25-2020); [Techstars mentors](https://www.techstars.com/mentors); [Techstars 2025 All-Star Mentors](https://www.techstars.com/blog/innovation-in-action/meet-the-2025-techstars-all-star-mentors)

- Techstars' declining "active mentor" counts (8,500 -> 1,500 over 2020-2026, likely definitional) hint that mentor networks need an "active" definition and periodic pruning rather than cumulative counts.

### Gaps

- 500 Global's current alumni Slack/perks could not be confirmed from an official current page.
